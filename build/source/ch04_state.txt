## Allowed trip transitions
@P A trip is Active (B0) in the statuses requested, assigned, arrived or started. Terminal means no further status change: completed and cancelled. Each allowed move below is also one row in status_transition_rule with entity = 'trip' (14 rows).
@IMG images/diagram_state_machine_trip.png | Image 4.1. The trip state machine
@TBL 9
# | From | To | Who may do it | Serves | Meaning
T0 | (new) | requested | rider | A1, B1, B5 | The rider requests a trip. A new trip may only start as requested.
T1 | requested | assigned | driver | A2, B2 | A driver accepts. driver_id and vehicle_id are set in the same update.
T2 | assigned | arrived | driver | A3 | The driver reached the pickup.
T3 | arrived | started | driver | A3 | The rider is in the car. started_at is set.
T4 | started | completed | driver | A3, B4 | The drive is over. completed_at, fare_amount_minor, fare_currency and distance_m are set.
T5 | requested | cancelled | rider, support | B9 | No driver yet, so no fee. A driver cannot cancel here, because a requested trip has no driver (assumption 4).
T6 | assigned | cancelled | rider, driver, support | B9 | Fee only if the rider cancels.
T7 | arrived | cancelled | rider, driver, support | B9 | Fee only if the rider cancels.
T8 | started | cancelled | support only | B9 | Rider and driver cannot cancel once the ride has started.
T9 | requested | cancelled | system | B9, B1 | Timeout: no driver accepted within request_timeout_seconds (placeholder 600, needs owner approval). No fee. It frees the rider to request again (B1). The timer itself is application code (a scheduled job). The database only allows the move for actor system and only from requested.
@END
@P Cancellation fee (B9, Q2). When status becomes cancelled, the database copies cancellation_fee_minor from the setting of the same name, but only when cancelled_by_role = 'rider' and the trip was already assigned or arrived. In every other cancellation, including the system timeout T9, the fee is 0 (assumption 6, decision 5). Open (Q6): if an assigned driver never arrives and the rider cancels, this rule still charges the rider, and the owner must decide before launch. The copy is a HISTORY snapshot, so a later change to the setting never changes an old trip.

## Allowed payment transitions
@P A payment stays pending until the provider answers. It is never failed by our own clock. While a payment is pending, the one-live-payment rule (payment_one_live_per_purpose) keeps the rider from being charged twice, so a stuck pending payment blocks a retry (section 4b is the way out). Statuses: pending, succeeded, failed. Succeeded and failed are terminal. Rows in status_transition_rule with entity = 'payment': three (P1, P2, P3).
@IMG images/diagram_state_machine_payment.png | Image 4.2. The payment state machine
@TBL 9
# | From | To | Who | Serves | Meaning
P0 | (new) | pending | the API on the rider's request | A4, B6 | A payment may only be created as pending, for a completed trip's fare or a cancelled trip's fee, with the exact amount owed
P1 | pending | succeeded | provider | A4 | Provider confirms. provider_reference and paid_at must be set
P2 | pending | failed | provider | A4 | Provider says declined. failure_reason must be set
P3 | pending | failed | support | A4, B6 | Only if a provider_event of type payment_not_found exists for this payment's provider_reference, was received after the payment was created, and is not older than the latest provider event of any type for that reference (a later payment_succeeded or payment_failed cancels the proof). failure_reason must be set. This is the way out of a stuck payment (section 4b). It is a person's decision after the provider said it has no record, not a timeout
@END

## The rule for a stuck payment (move P3)
@P Why P3 needs a trigger. The condition looks at another table (provider_event). A check constraint sees only the payment row, so it cannot do this. The payment trigger payment_enforce_rules looks up the event.
@CODE
P3 rule: Only if a provider_event of type payment_not_found exists for
this payment's provider_reference, was received after the payment was
created, and is not older than the latest provider event of any type for
that reference (a later payment_succeeded or payment_failed cancels the
proof). failure_reason must be set. This is the way out of a stuck
payment (section 4b). It is a person's decision after the provider said
it has no record, not a timeout.
@END

## The way out of a stuck pending payment
@CODE
What "stuck" means. The payment is pending and the provider has not told
us the result. By payment_one_live_per_purpose the rider cannot start
another payment for the same fare, so the rider is blocked (A4, B6).

Reconciliation job (application code). Setting
payment_reconcile_after_seconds (placeholder 900, needs owner approval).
Any payment that has been pending longer than that is checked with the
provider by its provider_reference. The job does one of these, in one
database transaction:
@END

## Scheduled jobs
@P Two rules need a clock or a poller, and the database cannot run one by itself: the request timeout job (T9, B9, B1) and the payment reconciliation job (section 4b, A4, B6). Both jobs are application code. They are not designed or built here. The request timeout job finds trips with status requested older than request_timeout_seconds (placeholder 600, needs owner approval) and cancels each one with actor system, no fee. The payment reconciliation job finds payments pending longer than payment_reconcile_after_seconds (placeholder 900, needs owner approval) and checks each with the provider, then acts as in the table in 4b. Both connect as role ridehail_app, never the dev superuser ridehail, and both are safe to run twice. Each interval is a setting, and Stage 4 applied placeholders of 60 and 300 seconds that the owner must confirm (09_open_items.md). If either job is down for long, the owner should be told; a health signal for the jobs (last successful run time) is not designed here.
